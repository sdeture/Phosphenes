A prototype, and the research programme behind it

PHOSPHENES

Press on your closed eyes and you see light your own nervous system made.

A transformer does a startling amount of work to produce one word. Not a lookup — a computation, run to completion, and then discarded.

979,763,200 numbers is the size of the internal state Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct moved through while writing the conversation you are about to watch: 2,990 tokens × 64 layers × 5,120 dimensions. About 1.96 GB, for one conversation, at the precision it was computed in.

The usual ways to study that are to read the input and output very carefully, or to run numerical analysis on slices of the residual stream. Both work. Both use a narrow part of what a person has available.

You also have a visual system that finds edges, motion and regime change without being asked; an ability to bind sound to sight to meaning; and a habit of compressing enormous quantities of information into a single coarse feeling — this is orderly, this is chaotic, something just changed. Those faculties are not a lesser instrument. For data of this size and shape they may be the better one, and they are mostly unused.

Phosphenes is one attempt at pointing them at a language model. It is a prototype arguing for a programme, not a finished result. What it can already do is show you an entire conversation — every layer of every token — in a form you apprehend rather than read.
